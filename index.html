Exhibition review

View issue

Evidências brings together images produced since 2022 from gestures of collection, accumulation, and transformation: a visual, almost archaeological, archive where obsessions and fascinations are revealed.

Starting from the practice of collecting traces and fragments of the outside world, the artist explores the tensions with the inner world in her first solo exhibition.

Between the documentary and the dreamlike, the photographs presented transform remnants and signs of existence into emotional and autobiographical objects, revealing a process of self-knowledge, transformation, and (re)construction of the self.

MEMORABÍLIA
nos bons lugares há sempre vento in good places there is always wind

Belonging to Memento series and to my collection, this animal's body is a recurring figure in my work, appearing across different images and objects as a persistent presence. Often associated with decay, death and what is discarded, it also carries a sense of survival, adaptation and persistence.

In nos bons lugares há sempre vento, this recurring figure appears dead, yet strangely alive within the reflection of the sky. The mirror displaces the animal from the earthly space it inhabits, while changing light continuously transforms its presence. It becomes suspended between states: body and image, death and persistence, disappearance and transcendence. The wind eventually carries it out of the frame, transforming the image of death into a movement towards elsewhere.

The work continues an ongoing encounter with the rat as a figure through which I approach death not as an end, but as a transformation into something.

DÉJÀ VU

Synopsis

A young woman searches for traces of herself through a prehistoric monument swallowed by urban space.

Driven by déjà vu, she returns to a place she grew up in, repeating gestures of observation, collection and revelation.

The film explores photography as an archaeological gesture and the relationship between memory, territory and identity.

ABOUT

Cíntia Ferreira (Lisbon, 1999) is a multidisciplinary artist working across photography, sculpture, film and performance.

Her practice explores memory, identity, territory and the archive through processes of collection, observation and reconstruction.

Her work often approaches photography as an archaeological gesture, investigating fragments, traces and vestiges as forms of personal and collective memory.
